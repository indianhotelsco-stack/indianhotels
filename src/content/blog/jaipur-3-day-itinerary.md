Jaipur rewards a slower pace than most itineraries give it. It's tempting to try to see the Pink City in a rushed day trip from Delhi, but the major sites — Amber Fort especially — deserve unhurried time, and the old city's bazaars are worth wandering rather than ticking off. Three days is a realistic minimum to see the highlights without feeling rushed.

## Day 1: Amber Fort and the Approach

Start early and dedicate the whole morning to **Amber Fort**, on a hilltop about 11km outside the city centre. This is Jaipur's single most impressive site — a sprawling complex of courtyards, mirrored halls (the Sheesh Mahal is the highlight) and fortifications with views back over the Maota Lake below. Arrive as close to opening as you can manage; by mid-morning the courtyards fill with tour groups and the experience changes considerably.

On the way back into the city, stop at **Jal Mahal**, the "Water Palace" that appears to float in the middle of Man Sagar Lake — you can't enter, but it's a five-minute photo stop that's genuinely worth the detour.

Spend the afternoon recovering from the morning's walking and, if you have energy, take a first pass through the old city bazaars near Hawa Mahal for orientation before the more focused shopping on Day 3.

## Day 2: City Palace, Jantar Mantar, and Hawa Mahal

Day two stays inside the old walled city, where the three most famous sites sit within easy walking distance of each other.

**City Palace** is still partly a residence of the former royal family and partly a museum — the courtyards, the armoury, and the costume galleries are the highlights. Budget two to three hours.

Right next door, **Jantar Mantar** is Jaipur's astronomical observatory, built in the early 18th century — a strange, striking collection of huge stone instruments that still function. It's a shorter visit (45–60 minutes is plenty for most people) but genuinely unlike anything else on a typical Rajasthan itinerary.

Finish the day at **Hawa Mahal**, the "Palace of Winds" — Jaipur's most photographed building. The interior is fairly modest and quick to see; the real draw is the honeycomb facade itself, best viewed and photographed from the street or a rooftop café directly opposite in the late afternoon light.

## Day 3: Bazaars, Nahargarh Fort, and Departure

Dedicate the morning to Jaipur's old city bazaars — **Johari Bazaar** for jewellery, **Bapu Bazaar** for textiles and leather juttis, **Chandpole Bazaar** for blue pottery and handicrafts. This is less a checklist and more a wander; the bazaars are genuinely part of the Jaipur experience rather than just a shopping errand.

If your schedule allows an extra half-day, **Nahargarh Fort**, on the hills above the city, is best visited in the late afternoon for sunset views over Jaipur — a quieter, less crowded alternative to Amber Fort for anyone who wants one more fort experience without the crowds.

## Practical Notes

Jaipur's heat is a real factor in itinerary planning — from April through June, midday temperatures make extended outdoor sightseeing genuinely uncomfortable, so front-loading outdoor sites (Amber Fort, Nahargarh) into the early morning matters more here than in most Indian cities. October to March is the far more comfortable window for a walking-heavy itinerary like this one.
