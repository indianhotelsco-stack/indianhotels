The "Golden Triangle" — Delhi, Agra, Jaipur — is the most-travelled first-timer route in India, and also the most-compressed. Plenty of tour packages try to fit it into 3–4 days, which is possible but rushed. Here's a more realistic breakdown depending on how much time you actually have.

## The Bare Minimum: 5 Days

Five days is tight but workable if this is a stop on a longer trip rather than the whole holiday:

- **Delhi:** 1.5 days — enough for Old Delhi (Red Fort, Jama Masjid, a rickshaw ride through Chandni Chowk) and New Delhi (Humayun's Tomb, India Gate, Qutub Minar) if you move efficiently.
- **Agra:** 1 day — a full day dedicated to the Taj Mahal (ideally at sunrise) plus Agra Fort in the afternoon. This is genuinely enough time if you don't need a second morning at the Taj.
- **Jaipur:** 2 days — the minimum to cover Amber Fort, City Palace, Hawa Mahal and Jantar Mantar without feeling completely rushed (see our [3-day Jaipur itinerary](/blog/jaipur-3-day-itinerary) for what a more relaxed version looks like).
- **Travel:** roughly half a day total is absorbed by the Delhi–Agra and Agra–Jaipur road/rail legs.

## The Comfortable Version: 7 Days

Adding two days changes the trip meaningfully rather than just padding it:

- **Delhi:** 2 days, allowing a genuinely unhurried split between Old and New Delhi with time to actually enjoy a meal or market rather than rushing between sites.
- **Agra:** 1.5–2 days — enough for both a sunrise and a sunset visit to the Taj Mahal (the light is different enough at each that many photographers consider both worthwhile), plus Agra Fort and possibly Fatehpur Sikri as a half-day trip.
- **Jaipur:** 3 days — the pacing in our [full Jaipur itinerary](/blog/jaipur-3-day-itinerary), with room to add Nahargarh Fort for sunset.

## The Relaxed Version: 10 Days

At 10 days, it's worth adding a fourth stop rather than just slowing the same three cities further. The most common additions are **Ranthambore** (tiger safari, roughly 2 days, sits conveniently between Agra and Jaipur) or extending into Rajasthan further — Udaipur or Jodhpur — which turns the "Triangle" into a broader Rajasthan loop.

## Which Direction to Travel

Nearly everyone does Delhi → Agra → Jaipur → back to Delhi (a genuine triangle by road), rather than doubling back through Delhi in the middle. If you're flying onward from Jaipur rather than returning to Delhi, this order also avoids unnecessary backtracking.

## Getting Between Cities

Delhi–Agra has a fast train option (under 2 hours on the Gatimaan Express) that's genuinely worth booking ahead rather than defaulting to a car for that leg. Agra–Jaipur is more commonly done by road (around 4–5 hours) since train options are slower and less frequent on that specific route.
