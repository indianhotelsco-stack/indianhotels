Booking a hotel in India for the first time raises a few questions that don't really come up in more heavily-standardised travel markets. Here's what's worth checking before you book, based on what actually trips up first-time visitors.

## Location Matters More Than the Photos Suggest

Indian cities can have significant traffic congestion, and "close on the map" doesn't always mean "close in practice" — a hotel 3km from a major sight can be a 10-minute walk or a 45-minute crawl through traffic depending on the route and time of day. Before booking, check the hotel's actual distance and typical travel time to the sites you care most about, not just the straight-line distance on a map.

For sunrise visits in particular (the Taj Mahal being the obvious example), staying within easy walking or short-drive distance of the relevant gate is worth prioritising over a marginally cheaper option further out — the value of an extra 30 minutes of sleep, or avoiding a pre-dawn taxi, is easy to underrate when booking from home.

## Air Conditioning Isn't Always a Given

Outside of the winter months (roughly December–February) and outside of hill stations, air conditioning is close to essential for a comfortable stay across most of India. Budget properties sometimes offer "AC" and "non-AC" room categories at different prices — double-check which you're booking, and don't assume a fan is sufficient outside the cooler months.

## Check What "Breakfast Included" Actually Means

This varies more than you'd expect — some properties include a full buffet, others a much more limited continental spread, and the difference matters if breakfast is a meaningful part of your daily food budget. Recent reviews usually mention this specifically if it's worth flagging.

## Water Pressure and Hot Water Timing

At budget and some mid-range properties, hot water may come from a tank heater switched on shortly before use rather than being always-on — worth asking about if you're arriving late at night or leaving very early and want a shower either side. This is one of the most common sources of minor first-trip frustration and one of the easiest to avoid by simply asking ahead or checking recent reviews.

## Confirm Airport or Station Transfers Separately

Many hotels offer transfers, but "offer" doesn't always mean "included" — confirm whether it's complimentary or paid, and get a price in advance if it's the latter, rather than negotiating on arrival when you have less leverage and less local knowledge of a fair rate.

## Festivals and Peak Dates Change Everything

Major festivals (Diwali, Holi, and regional festivals specific to a state) can significantly affect both hotel prices and availability, and can also change practical things like which restaurants are open or how accessible certain sites are. If your trip coincides with a major festival, check specifically what that means for your itinerary rather than assuming it's business as usual with better decorations.

## Don't Over-Optimise for the Cheapest Option

The price gap between a well-reviewed budget hotel and a genuinely comfortable mid-range option is often smaller in absolute terms than it looks in percentage terms, especially once you factor in a full trip's worth of nights. For a first trip to India, the extra reliability of mid-range (see our [budget vs luxury breakdown](/blog/budget-vs-luxury-hotels-india)) is often worth the modest premium, particularly for your first and last nights when jet lag makes a smooth, low-friction stay especially valuable.
