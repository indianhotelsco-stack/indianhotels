The Taj Mahal is one of the few monuments that genuinely looks different depending on when you see it — the marble shifts from pink at dawn to blinding white at midday to soft gold at sunset, and the crowd size and heat change just as dramatically depending on the season. Here's how the year actually breaks down.

## October to March: The Reliable Window

This is when the vast majority of international visitors come, and for good reason. Daytime temperatures sit comfortably between 15–25°C, the air is generally clearer (less of the summer haze that can dull photos), and mornings are cool enough that an early visit doesn't feel like an endurance test.

December and January are the coolest months, occasionally cold enough for morning fog that can delay the "first light" views photographers chase — if fog is a risk, plan for a mid-morning visit instead of sunrise, or build in a buffer day. February and March are usually the sweet spot: still cool, but past the fog risk, and before the pre-monsoon heat builds.

The trade-off is crowds. This window is peak season, and the Taj gets genuinely busy, especially on Fridays (when it's closed to the public for prayers at the mosque, so don't plan for that day at all) and around Indian public holidays.

## April to June: Hot, Bright, and Quiet

Agra summers are intense — daytime temperatures regularly exceed 40°C. If you can handle the heat, this is when the site is at its quietest, and the hazy summer light can actually produce striking, almost monochrome photos of the marble against a pale sky. A sunrise visit is close to essential in this window, both for comfort and for the best light before the sun gets high and harsh.

## July to September: Monsoon

Monsoon season brings dramatic skies, cooler air between downpours, and some of the lowest visitor numbers of the year. The garden is at its greenest. The trade-off is unpredictability — a sudden downpour can end a visit early, and humidity is high throughout.

## Time of Day Matters as Much as Time of Year

Regardless of season, the Taj Mahal is genuinely a different experience at different times of day:

- **Sunrise** — the softest light, the smallest crowds, and the classic "pink marble" effect as the sun comes up. Gates typically open around 6am; arriving at opening is the single best way to beat both heat and crowds year-round.
- **Midday** — the marble reads as stark white, harsher for photography but useful if you want to see architectural detail without the golden-hour glow.
- **Sunset** — a second good window for photography, generally quieter than sunrise since most tour groups prioritise the morning slot.
