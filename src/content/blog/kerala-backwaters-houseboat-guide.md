A houseboat cruise is the single most-recommended thing to do in Kerala, and also one of the most-misunderstood — first-time visitors often aren't sure how long to book, what's actually included, or whether it's worth the price. Here's a practical rundown.

## What a Houseboat Actually Is

Kerala's houseboats (locally called *kettuvallam*) were originally rice barges, converted over the past few decades into tourist accommodation. They range from simple one-bedroom boats to multi-bedroom vessels with air conditioning, private decks and full kitchens. Nearly all include a crew — a driver and a cook are standard even on modest boats — so this isn't self-navigated; you're a passenger on a slow, scenic drift through the canals.

## How Long to Book

**The overnight cruise is the standard, and for good reason.** A day-trip houseboat (roughly 4–6 hours) covers the basics — palm-lined canals, paddy fields, village life along the banks — but the overnight version adds a sunset on the water, a home-cooked Kerala meal, and an early morning cruise before the day-trippers are out, which is when the backwaters are at their calmest and most atmospheric.

Multi-day cruises exist but are a niche choice — one night covers the experience well for most visitors, and the canals you cover on a two-night trip aren't dramatically different from a one-night route.

## Alleppey vs Kumarakom

The two main departure points have slightly different characters:

- **Alleppey (Alappuzha)** is the classic, busiest departure point, with the widest choice of boats and operators, and the most "iconic" narrow-canal scenery close to the town.
- **Kumarakom**, on Vembanad Lake, tends to feel a little quieter and more spread out, with more of the cruise on open lake rather than narrow canals — a different, more spacious kind of scenic.

Neither is objectively better; Alleppey suits people who want maximum canal scenery and choice, Kumarakom suits people who prefer a calmer lake setting and don't mind slightly less variety in operators.

## What's Usually Included

Most houseboat packages are all-inclusive for the duration: crew, all meals (breakfast, lunch, dinner, sometimes afternoon snacks), and the boat itself. Alcohol is typically not included and often can't be brought onboard depending on the operator — worth checking directly if that matters to you. Air conditioning is common on mid-range and above boats but isn't universal on the most basic ones, which matters more than you'd think given Kerala's humidity.

## When to Go

**November to February** is the most comfortable window — dry, warm without being oppressive, and the paddy fields are green. March to May gets hot and humid. The monsoon (June to September) actually has its own appeal — dramatically green scenery and lower prices — but rain can disrupt the open-deck experience that's a big part of the appeal.

## Booking Tips

Book through your hotel or a reputable local operator rather than a random tout at the jetty — quality varies hugely and it's hard to verify a boat's actual condition (AC working, clean linens, safety equipment) without a recommendation you can trust. If you're staying at a hotel near the backwaters, ask at check-in; most can arrange a houseboat directly.
